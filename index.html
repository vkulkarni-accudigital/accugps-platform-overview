01 · How far

Movement & Distance

Where did the vehicles go, and how far? Four reports answer it at different resolutions: a full daily Summary, a stop-by-stop Travel log, a distance Mileage chart, and a map-based Route analysis.

2.1

Summary

Table

The most complete single report. Pick a tracker and a range and every day comes back as a row with driving, fuel, stops, and speed all in one place. It is the report to read first when you're not sure which one you need.

01Opening Summary · pick the tracker and range from the Reports menuLive report
02Reading Summary · one row per day, every metric side by side
  • Travel time & Fuel. Total driving time and gallons consumed for the day.
  • Stop counts. Total stops split into 0–30 min, 30 min–2 h, and longer bands, separating short errands from long dwells.
  • Average & Max speed. The day's mean speed and its single fastest reading.
  • Speed bands. Percent of time in each range: 0–5, 5–20, 20–45, 45–75, and ≥75 mph.
2.2

Travel

Timeline

Where Summary aggregates, Travel narrates. It lays the day out as a timeline of alternating drives and stops, each with a time, a duration, and, for stops, a street address.

03Travel · departures, stops, and addresses down the day
  • Driving segments. Every leg with its own departure time, duration, and distance.
  • Stops. Numbered markers with dwell time and the exact address where the vehicle sat.
  • Daily totals. Total Distance, Total Stopped Time, and Total Traveled Time.
2.3

Mileage

Bar chart

A clean bar chart of miles driven each day across the range. Tall bars are heavy days, gaps are idle days: the shape of the month in one look, ideal for spotting utilization patterns.

04Mileage · daily distance across the selected range
2.4

Route analysis

Map

Route analysis draws the real routes vehicles took on the map. Unlike the single-tracker reports, it takes a time range and lets you tick multiple trackers at once, which is useful for comparing coverage or auditing a service area.

05Setup · pick a time range and the trackers to plot
06Plotted · each vehicle's real route on the map
ROI

Distance is the denominator behind cost-per-mile, billing, and utilization. Seeing it four ways (summed, sequenced, charted, and mapped) lets you bill accurately, right-size the fleet, and settle route disputes with the actual path driven.

02 · How long

Time & Labor

Distance tells you how far; these reports tell you how the hours were spent · driving, idling, engine-on, and on the clock. They turn the tracker into a timekeeper for payroll, idle-cost control, and hours-of-service compliance.

3.1

Driving time

Bar chart

A daily bar chart that separates three states so you can see not just whether a vehicle worked but how. Excessive idle or engine-on time with little driving is wasted fuel and wear.

07Driving time · Driving, Idle, and Orange-wire-on hours per day
DRIVING

In motion

Hours the vehicle was actually moving down the road.

IDLE

Stopped, running

Engine on but not moving. The number to drive down.

ORANGE WIRE

Engine on

Total ignition-on time, the envelope around driving + idle.

3.2

Work Hour

Export

Work Hour turns movement into a labor record. Choose a From / To date span and a Daily Starting Time, tick the drivers (or All Trackers), and Export. The report derives each day's working hours from first movement to last stop.

08Work Hour · date span, start time, driver selection, Export
  • From / To. The pay period or any date span you want to total.
  • Daily Starting Time. Sets when each work day begins so overnight shifts land on the right day.
  • Driver selection. Tick All Trackers or individual drivers, then Export for the timesheet.
Payroll-readyBecause hours come from the vehicle itself, Work Hour gives an objective record to check against submitted timesheets, with no rounding and no guesswork.
3.3

Orange wire

Timeline

The "orange wire" is powered whenever the engine is on. This report lists each engine-on and idle period down the day with its address, and totals them at the bottom, a location-aware view of where time and fuel go.

09Orange wire · engine-on and idle periods by location
  • Per-period rows. Each engine-on and idle span stamped with time and street address.
  • Totals. Total Orange wire on and Total Idle Time for the range.
  • Idle filter. Merge brief shutdowns below a chosen threshold to clean up the timeline.
3.4

E-log

Log

The E-log report holds electronic duty-status records for hours-of-service compliance: the digital logbook regulated drivers keep. Configuration and driver assignment for ELD are covered in the Administration guide.

10E-log · hours-of-service duty-status records
ROI

Idle time burns fuel and engine life for zero miles; unverified timesheets inflate payroll. Measuring both against the vehicle's own record turns soft costs into hard, correctable numbers. Cut idling, pay for hours actually worked.

03 · How much

Cost & Compliance

These reports speak the language of the finance and compliance desks: fuel burned, taxes owed, and time spent at key sites. They convert location data into the numbers that go on filings and invoices.

4.1

Fuel

Bar chart

A daily bar chart of gallons consumed. Read it alongside the Mileage chart: high fuel with low miles points at idling or heavy loads, while the ratio of the two is effectively your MPG trend over the range.

11Fuel · daily gallons consumed
Set the baseline firstFuel figures depend on each unit's city and highway fuel economy, set at the unit level in Settings, covered in the Reporting Settings section below.
4.2

IFTA (State Miles)

Table

For fleets that cross state lines, IFTA reporting requires miles driven per jurisdiction. This report breaks the range's distance out by state · ready to drop into a quarterly fuel-tax filing.

12IFTA · state-by-state mileage for the range
Filing made simpleExport to Excel or CSV to feed your IFTA return directly. The platform tracks the state boundaries so you don't reconstruct them from a mileage log.
4.3

Geo-fence visit

Table

Pick a geofence (a yard, a job site, a customer) and a From / To range, and the report lists every vehicle that entered, with arrival, departure, and dwell time for each visit.

13Geo-fence visit · arrivals, departures, and dwell time
  • Geo-fence. Choose which fence to report on from the dropdown.
  • Visit from / Stay until. Timestamped arrival and departure for each visit.
  • Duration. Exact dwell time: the number for site-time billing or SLA checks.
ROI

Fuel and taxes are among the largest line items a fleet carries, and site-time is often billable. Pulling all three straight from tracker data means defensible IFTA filings, tighter fuel oversight, and invoices backed by exact dwell times.

04 · How healthy

Vehicle Health

The last pair of reports looks inward, at the machines themselves, tracking service intervals so nothing gets missed, and surfacing the engine's own diagnostic trouble codes the moment they appear.

5.1

Maintenance

Table

A per-asset table of odometer and engine hours, both Total (lifetime) and Current (since last service), with controls to define what "due" means and to log what's been done.

14The table · odometer & engine hours per asset
15Intervals & history · set what "due" means, log services
  • Total vs. Current. Lifetime odometer/engine time beside the counters since the last service reset.
  • Service Interval. Set the mileage or hour threshold at which each service becomes due.
  • Service history. A running log of completed services per asset for warranty and resale records.
Preventive, not reactiveSetting intervals turns Maintenance from a record into an early-warning system: oil changes, inspections, and PM come due before a breakdown, not after.
5.2

DTC

Cards + PDF

When the engine throws a fault, the OBD device reads its Diagnostic Trouble Code. The DTC report groups codes into the four standard families so you can tell at a glance which system is affected, and exports a PDF for the shop.

05 · Behind the numbers

Reporting Settings

The Fuel and Summary reports can only be as accurate as the vehicles behind them. Before you trust a gallon figure, set each unit's fuel economy · because AccuGPS derives fuel from distance driven, the platform needs to know how far each vehicle goes on a gallon in the city and on the highway.

Open Settings › Reporting. Pick a Tracker from the dropdown, then enter its two fuel-economy figures. The reports immediately use these rates to turn miles into gallons.

17Settings › Reporting · city & highway MPG set per unit
CITY

Fuel economy (city)

Miles per gallon in stop-and-go driving: the rate applied to low-speed, high-idle time.

HIGHWAY

Fuel economy (hwy)

Miles per gallon at cruising speed: the rate applied to sustained highway travel.

SCOPE

Apply to all trackers

Set one vehicle, then push the same rates across the whole fleet in a click.

  • Per-unit accuracy. Every tracker carries its own city and highway MPG, so a cargo van and a pickup aren't estimated with the same rate.
  • Apply to all trackers. For a uniform fleet, enter the numbers once and tick Apply to all trackers to copy them everywhere.
  • Save to apply. Press Save and the Fuel report, plus fuel figures in Summary, recalculate on the new rates.
Why this matters firstSkip this step and fuel numbers default to a generic estimate. Two minutes per unit here is the difference between a fuel report you can put in front of finance and one you can't.
06 · On autopilot

Scheduled Report Tasks

Every report so far you ran by hand. A Report Task runs one for you on a schedule and emails it out · so the Monday mileage recap or the daily driver timesheet lands in the right inboxes without anyone opening the platform.

From Settings › Report Tasks, press Add to build a task. Choose the report, the trackers it covers, how often it runs, and who receives it.

18Setting a Report Task · report, trackers, frequency & recipients
  1. Pick the Report to send, e.g. Summary, Mileage, or Work Hour.
  2. Select the Trackers it covers: tick All Trackers or choose specific vehicles from the grid.
  3. Set the Time · how often it runs, such as Every day.
  4. Add up to 5 recipient emails, then Save · the task appears in the list, ready to edit or remove.
WHAT

Any report

Schedule the same library you run by hand, and the task delivers it on time.

WHEN

On a cadence

Recurring delivery, e.g. every day, so recaps arrive without a reminder.

WHO

Up to 5 inboxes

Route each report to the people who act on it: dispatch, finance, or a client.

Set once, runs foreverA handful of tasks (a daily timesheet to payroll, a weekly mileage recap to the owner, a monthly IFTA pull to the accountant) turns reporting from a chore into something that just shows up.
ROI

Accurate fuel rates plus automated delivery mean the numbers are both right and in front of the people who need them · without a manager remembering to pull a single report. The fleet reports on itself.
